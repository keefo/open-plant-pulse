How it has held

Humidity and the fan on one axis, because both are percentages and the only way to judge a controller is to see the two together. The fan is drawn as steps: it holds whatever it was last told until it is told otherwise.

Nothing recorded yet. The fan is written down when it changes.

Control

The enclosure holds humidity well, which is why the begonias are in it and why it reaches 86 % on a closed afternoon. The way down is the exhaust fan, and this holds it at a number instead of somebody typing SET_FAN_SPEED when they notice. It steps when a reading arrives rather than on a timer, stands down while the printer is printing, and switches the fan off if the sensor goes quiet — acting on an old humidity is guessing.

Tuning

Kp acts on how far off it is now. Ki acts on how long it has been off, per minute. Kd is off by default: two readings minutes apart, each rounded to a tenth, are mostly noise. The slowest speed is a floor, not a threshold: below it a fan tends to buzz without moving air, so when less than that is wanted the fan runs at its floor for part of a pulse window and stops for the rest — the trick a slow PWM plays on a heater. It stops entirely only once the box is back to target.

The printer

Alerts to a phone

Everything else the hub says, it says on a page somebody has to open. Water on a floor is different: it matters most to whoever is out, and a notice nobody sees until they get home is not a notice. Install ntfy on the phone, subscribe it to a topic nobody else would guess, and paste the same address here — https://ntfy.sh/<topic>, or the same path on a server of your own. Anyone who knows the topic can read it and post to it, so treat the address as the secret it is. Left empty, the hub sends nothing.

What is sent

A leak sensor finding water sends at once, at the priority that rings a silenced phone. It is sent on the change rather than the state: these sensors repeat themselves while they are wet, and a phone that buzzes every few minutes until somebody gets home is a phone that gets silenced. Drying out is sent too, quietly.

Add a light

The hub switches each light in whatever way its type needs, on the hours set here. Which plants stand under a light, and how much of it reaches each of them, is set on those plants' configuration pages.

A light is one switch, so its hours belong to it rather than to any one plant beneath it — a panel over a bench keeps the same window for everything under it. Times are this hub’s local time, and a window may run past midnight.

A time of day makes one day comparable with the next. An interval draws the drying curve in more detail.

Weighings recorded

Weigh the pot whenever you like. A pot that suddenly weighs much more has been watered, so the weighing is recorded as one; the hub says so before saving, and a mistyped number can be cancelled there. Tick Just fertilized for the one thing the weight cannot reveal; it is written at the weighing’s own date, so a weighing entered later is filed on the day it happened, and it does not change the weights below. Thirsty at is what this pot weighs when this plant wants water: high for one that should never dry out, low for one that likes to. Set it once, from the scale or from memory, and the hub can say how long the water will last. The pot's dry weight is not asked for: it is the lightest this pot has ever been weighed, which is the driest it is known to get. The chart runs from full down to that, green above the thirsty weight and warming through to red below it, so a glance says how wet the pot is rather than only how much of this watering is left.

      EVAPOTRANSPIRATION

      Water use

      --Typical a day
      --Range a day
      --Used in all
      --Most recent rate
      Millilitres a day · each bar is one span between weighings

      Weigh the pot twice, a day or more apart, and its water use shows here.

      What the pot loses between two weighings, with nothing poured in, is water gone through the leaves and off the soil: evapotranspiration, measured the way a weighing lysimeter measures it. A gram of water is a millilitre, so the scale reads in the units the watering can does. Each bar is the average over its own span, because five days and 400 ml says 80 ml a day and says nothing about which day was thirstiest. Spans containing a watering are left out: water went in as well as out, and one weighing cannot separate them.

      Where a sensor reports the air of the room this plant is in, the rates are also held against its vapour pressure deficit — how hard the air is pulling water out of things. The same plant drinks two or three times as much in warm dry air as in cool damp air, so dividing that out leaves the plant's own part of the change. The air belongs to the room rather than to the sensor, so one probe on a windowsill speaks for every pot on it.
